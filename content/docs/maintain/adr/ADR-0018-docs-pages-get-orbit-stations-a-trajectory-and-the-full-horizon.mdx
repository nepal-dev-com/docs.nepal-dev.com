## Context

ADR-0017 made the whole site one solar system, but the two columns a reader uses most on a docs
page still look like Fumadocs' defaults with a dot added:

- **The left sidebar** is Fumadocs' list: folders as plain rows with a straight guide line, the
  current page marked by a dot. The metaphor stops at the planet chip.
- **The right-hand "On this page"** is Fumadocs' `normal` table of contents. Its rail curves only
  where heading depth changes (`components/toc/default.js` draws a cubic curve between items of
  different depth and a straight line otherwise), so on a page whose headings are all `##` it is
  one straight line. The owner remembers it as "tech-like" and curvy, and finds it plain now.
- **The horizon** below docs pages drops its scene and closing line (ADR-0017 decision 8). The
  owner wants the full horizon on every page.

The owner chose, from options with previews (2026-11-01): **orbit stations** for the left
sidebar, a **trajectory with a probe** for "On this page", and the **full horizon** on docs
pages.

Facts this decision rests on, read from the installed Fumadocs 16.17.9:

- The notebook layout's `sidebar.components` accepts `Item`, `Folder` and `Separator`
  components (`SidebarPageTreeComponents`, `components/sidebar/page-tree.d.ts`). The default
  renderer opens a folder when the current page is inside it (`active: path.includes(node)`),
  and otherwise follows `defaultOpenLevel`, 0 by default, so other folders start closed.
- `DocsPage` takes `slots.toc` with `provider`, `main` and `popover`
  (`layouts/notebook/page/index.d.ts`); `main` is the desktop column and `popover` the bar on
  narrow screens.
- `fumadocs-core/toc` exports `useItems`, `useActiveAnchors` and `AnchorProvider`; each item has
  `title`, `url` and `depth`.
- Each page's `structuredData` (fumadocs-mdx) holds its headings and text, so its word count is
  known at build time without reading the file again.

## Decision

1. **The sidebar top becomes one planet row.** The planet chip (mark and name, linked to the
   overview) and the day/night switch share one row: the switch shrinks to two icon buttons, a
   drawn sun for Use and a drawn moon for Maintain, each with its full name as its accessible
   name and tooltip. The moon picker sits under it on Use, as today.

2. **Every section is an orbit station.** A sidebar folder renders as a station: a card
   (12px radius, 1px border, the panel colour) with a header and its pages.
   - **Header:** a small orbit drawing (a ring, with a filled planet on it when the current page
     is inside), the section's name, the number of pages in it, and a chevron. When the folder
     has an index page, the name links to it, as Fumadocs does today.
   - **Pages are moons.** Inside an open station each page is a row with a moon dot, and the
     dots sit on one arc of the station's orbit, drawn in the left gutter: the arc bows outward,
     so a long station is a gentle curve, never a ruled line. The current page is the planet: an
     accent dot with a halo, its row tinted with the entry's ecosystem colour, marked
     `aria-current="page"`. Hovering a row fills its dot.
   - **Open and closed.** The station holding the current page opens and its border takes the
     ecosystem colour; the others start closed and show only their header, so a long sidebar
     (this site's Maintain track has twelve sections) scans as a list of station names. The
     chevron opens and closes a station.
   - **Nested folders** are smaller stations inside their parent, without a card border: a
     smaller orbit drawing and a shorter arc. Deeper levels indent without a further drawing.
   - **Top-level pages** outside any folder (an overview) sit above the stations on their own
     short arc, with the same moon dots. A Fumadocs separator is a dotted line with its label.

3. **"On this page" is a trajectory with a probe.** Our own component replaces Fumadocs'
   `normal` table of contents in both `slots.toc.main` and `slots.toc.popover`, built on
   `useItems` and `useActiveAnchors`.
   - **The path.** One continuous curved path through a dot per heading, drawn in a 28px gutter.
     Depth sets each dot's offset (`##` 6px, `###` 18px, deeper 26px). Between two headings of
     the same depth the path bows outward in a shallow arc; across a depth change it swings in an
     S-curve. So the path curves on every page, including one with only `##` headings.
   - **Read and unread.** The stretch already read is drawn solid in the accent colour, and its
     dots filled. The stretch ahead is drawn dashed and muted, with hollow dots, the same dashed
     language as an unreleased moon.
   - **The probe.** A small planet (accent, with a halo and a shaded night side) rides the path
     at the reader's position, gliding between dots as they scroll through a section, not jumping
     from heading to heading. The active heading's text takes the foreground colour.
   - **Time left.** The header reads "On this page" and, at the right, "4 min left": the page's
     reading time, from its word count at build time at 220 words a minute, scaled by how much
     is still unread and rounded up. At the end of the page it reads "End of page". A page under
     one minute shows no time.
   - **Back to top.** Under the list, "Back to top ↑", shown once the reader has scrolled past
     the first heading.
   - **On phones**, Fumadocs' bar under the header becomes our bar: a small ring with the probe
     at the reading position, the current heading, and the time left. Tapping it opens the same
     trajectory as a sheet; choosing a heading scrolls there and closes it.
   - Choosing a heading scrolls to it smoothly, and instantly under reduced motion, where the
     probe also jumps from dot to dot instead of gliding.

4. **The full horizon closes every page** (supersedes ADR-0017 decision 8, in part). Docs pages
   get the same footer as the home page: the closing line, the star rising over the planet's
   edge, the planet row and the link row, across the full width below the sidebar and the page.
   Its last link reads "Back to top" on docs pages and "Back to orbit" on the home page and the
   404. The `compact` variant is removed.

5. **What stays.** The moon picker, version notices, planet header, page headings, body text
   and reading column are unchanged. Search, the header and the theme switch are unchanged.
   Every drawing is inline SVG in the existing tokens (ADR-0017 decision 13); no dependency is
   added.

## Options considered

| Concern | Option | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Left sidebar | A — orbit stations | Each section reads as a place in the system; closed stations make long sidebars short | A custom `Folder` and `Item` to own; a card per section takes more height when open | Chosen (owner) |
| Left sidebar | B — planet console: the moons as the version picker, a sliding terminator for Use and Maintain, one continuous path through every page | The strongest use of the metaphor | The most custom code; replaces Fumadocs' version menu | Rejected |
| Left sidebar | C — refined classic: today's structure, polished | Lowest risk | Changes little; the metaphor still stops at the chip | Rejected |
| On this page | A — trajectory with a probe and time left | Curves on every page; shows progress and what is left | Our own component in place of Fumadocs' | Chosen (owner) |
| On this page | B — orbit dial: a reading-progress orbit above a curved list | Compact and playful | Progress and the list sit apart | Rejected |
| On this page | C — Fumadocs' `clerk` style, recoloured | No custom code | Least distinctive; still Fumadocs' look | Rejected |
| Horizon on docs pages | A — the full horizon | One footer everywhere | A long page ends with a tall footer | Chosen (owner) |
| Horizon on docs pages | B — the scene without the closing line | Shorter | Two footers to keep | Rejected |
| Reading time | A — word count at build time from `structuredData` | No work in the browser; the same number in every visit | Code blocks count as words | Chosen |
| Reading time | B — count the rendered text in the browser | Counts exactly what is shown | Work on every visit; differs before hydration | Rejected |

## Consequences

**Positive**

- Both columns carry the metaphor: sections are stations, pages are moons, reading is a
  trajectory.
- "On this page" curves on every page and tells the reader how far they are and how long is
  left.
- Long sidebars, such as this site's Maintain track, collapse to station names.
- One footer on every page.

**Negative / accepted trade-offs**

- Two Fumadocs parts are replaced by our own (the sidebar's folder and item, the table of
  contents and its phone bar). A Fumadocs upgrade that changes those slots needs a check.
- An open station takes more height than a plain folder.
- The full horizon adds height to the end of every docs page.
- Reading time is an estimate; long code blocks overstate it.

## Content invariants

- **3 (Version on every page)** and **5 (Readable as Markdown)**: unchanged. The trajectory and
  stations are page chrome; the per-page Markdown, `llms.txt` and `llms-full.txt` don't include
  them.
- No other invariant is touched; no content changes.

## Security considerations

None. This change touches no data, no endpoint and no secret. The reading time is computed at
build time from the page's own public text; nothing new leaves the system, and nothing is stored
in the browser.

## Implementation notes

- [x] `src/lib/trajectory.ts`: the path builder (dot offsets by depth, arcs and S-curves between
      dots, the length at each dot), the probe's position, the time-left function, and the
      sidebar's station arcs (`arcRow`, `arcSlot`). Pure functions, no React.
- [x] `src/lib/reading-time.ts`: words in a page's `structuredData`, and its minutes at 220 a
      minute. Found on implementation: fumadocs-mdx structures headings, paragraphs, quotes,
      tables and JSX blocks, not code blocks, so code does not count towards the reading time.
- [x] `src/components/docs/trajectory-toc.tsx` (client): the desktop column and the phone bar,
      passed as `slots.toc.main` and `slots.toc.popover` from `src/app/(site)/[...slug]/page.tsx`,
      which also passes the page's minutes through `ReadingTimeProvider`. Fumadocs' `TOCProvider`
      stays the provider. The reading position is measured from the headings' places on the page
      (the probe needs how far between two headings the reader is), so it uses `useTOCItems`
      rather than `useActiveAnchors`.
- [x] `src/components/docs/orbit-station.tsx` (client): `OrbitStation`, `OrbitItem` and
      `OrbitSeparator`, passed as `sidebar.components` in `src/app/(site)/layout.tsx`, built on
      Fumadocs' sidebar primitives (`fumadocs-ui/components/sidebar/base`) so opening, closing
      and the drawer keep working.
- [x] `src/components/docs/track-switch.tsx`: the planet row (decision 1); the banner carries
      `data-ring`, from which the sidebar takes its ecosystem colour.
- [x] `src/app/(site)/layout.tsx`: the full `HorizonFooter` with `backLabel="Back to top"`;
      `compact` removed from `src/components/horizon-footer.tsx`.
- [x] `src/app/site.css`: station, trajectory, probe and phone bar styles, with reduced-motion
      and `hover: none` rules; the orbit-line and dot overrides on Fumadocs' list and table of
      contents removed.
- [ ] Tests (ADR-0007): no runner is installed yet. The pure functions in `trajectory.ts` and
      `reading-time.ts` were checked by hand with a throwaway script (not committed): offsets by
      depth, an arc between equal depths and an S-curve across a change, one heading with no
      path, no headings, the probe before the first heading, between two and at the last, time
      left at 0%, 50% and 100%, before hydration and under a minute, the station arc's bow, ends
      and slots, and the reading minutes. They become the first tests once the runner's ADR is
      approved.
- [x] Checked in the built HTML: twelve stations on this site's Maintain pages with the current
      one open and marked, the current page with `aria-current="page"`, the trajectory column and
      phone bar, and the full horizon with its closing line and scene. Not yet looked at in a
      browser.
- [ ] `bun run lint` is blocked by typescript-eslint and TypeScript 7 (reported);
      `bun run types:check` and `bunx next build` pass.
