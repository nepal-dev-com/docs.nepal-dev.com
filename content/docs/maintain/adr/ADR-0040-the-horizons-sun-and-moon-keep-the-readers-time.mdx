## Context

- The owner asked for four things in the Horizon: a new sun and moon, the moon in dark mode and the sun in light mode, both placed by
  the current time and the lighting to follow them, keeping the way they rise with the scroll.
- What the code does now, read from `horizon-footer.tsx`, `horizon-sky.ts` and `site.css`:
  - both themes show one flat star disc at (690, 128) with a flat crescent above it at (690, 54), the flag's moon over its sun;
  - `.horizon-rise` lifts the disc and its glow 100 units through a scroll-driven animation, and the red faces fade in the same way;
  - the sky tint, the glow and the ranges are fixed per theme.
- Every page is prerendered (ADR-0016), so the server cannot know the hour. Only the reader's browser can. `HorizonAwake`, a client
  component, already runs on every footer.
- The red faces come from the logo and are drawn on the mountains' right sides, so light from the left cannot move them.
- On a 320px phone the scene shows about x=371 to x=829, which limits how wide a path across the sky can be (ADR-0037 decision 11).
- No state diagram is written: the parts of the day are a function of the clock, with no guarded transitions. No DFD is written: nothing
  crosses a boundary.

## Decision

1. **The theme chooses the body:** a sun in the light theme, a moon in the dark one. Both are in the page's HTML and CSS shows one, so
   switching theme swaps them at once, at the same hour.
2. **The reader's clock sets the hour.** It comes from the device's local time: no location prompt, and nothing is sent or stored.
3. **Each crosses the sky for twelve hours.**
   - The sun crosses from 6:00 to 18:00, the moon from 18:00 to 6:00, left to right, on one arc: from (400, 150), up to (600, 44) at the
     halfway hour, down to (800, 150).
   - The flag stays in front. From about 12:00 to 14:00 (and 0:00 to 2:00 for the moon) it flies across the disc, and is never covered
     (ADR-0037 decision 5).
4. **The other twelve hours, it rests low and stays in sky.**
   - The sun stays at (800, 150) from 18:00 to midnight, then at (400, 150) until 6:00. The moon stays at (800, 150) from 6:00 to noon,
     then at (400, 150) until 18:00.
   - It is dim while it rests, its glow small. At midnight (noon for the moon) it fades out on the right and in on the left over a second.
     You always see the body your theme asks for, and its place still tells the time.
5. **The scroll parallax stays, for both.** Each rises 100 units with the scroll into its place for the hour, and its glow grows with it,
   as the star does now. Scrolling back lowers it.
6. **They are drawn natural, not from the flag.**
   - The sun is a soft round disc with no hard edge and a wide halo, deep orange when low and pale gold when high.
   - The moon is a pale disc with a few soft darker seas and shaded edges. Its light part is **today's real phase**, worked out from the
     date alone, drawn as seen from the Himalaya: waxing is lit on the right.
   - On the nights around a new moon its dark side still shows faintly, so the dark theme is never without a moon.
   - Gradients and flat shapes only, with no blur filters, because a body that moves must not redraw a blur (ADR-0030 decision 3).
   - The sidebar's sun and moon keep the flag's forms (ADR-0037 decision 13).
7. **The lighting follows the hour.** Four fixed parts of the day, the same in every season and place because no location is known,
   blending across their edges:

   | Part | Hours | Sky and cloud undersides | Rock and snow |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Dawn | 5:00 to 7:00 | Rose gold | A warm tint on the snow |
   | Day | 7:00 to 17:00 | Clear, pale | True colours |
   | Dusk | 17:00 to 19:00 | Orange | A warm tint on the snow |
   | Night | 19:00 to 5:00 | Deep blue | A cool tint on both |

   - **The theme still rules.** The light theme at night dims only inside the scene, to a dusky blue. The dark theme by day lifts only
     a little and stays dark. The closing line, the planet row and the links do not change, so their contrast does not either.
   - **Never relit:** the flag's colours (ADR-0036 decision 7) and the entries' lights. The stars and shooting stars stay as they are.
8. **The red faces follow the light.** They are strongest when the light is low and on their side, and fade when it moves left: full for
   the sun low on the right, about 60% at its highest, down to 20% on the left and while it rests at night. Never below 20%, so the logo's
   mountain stays recognisable. Under the moon they reach at most 55%, cooler. This multiplies with the scroll's fade-in (ADR-0037
   decision 4), which stays.
9. **The rules of motion hold** (ADR-0030 decision 3).
   - Only transform and opacity animate, in CSS. The hour's values are set once when the page loads, once a minute while the footer is on
     screen and when a hidden tab returns; they are not animated. A minute's move is under a unit, too small to see.
   - No dependency is added.
10. **When it can't run.**
    - **No scripts:** the sun or moon by theme, at (690, 128) as the star stands now, with today's lighting and a fixed crescent moon.
    - **Reduced motion:** placed and lit by the hour, with no rise and no fade at the crossing.
    - **No scroll-driven animations:** placed and lit by the hour, standing risen.
11. **Where it lives.** `src/lib/horizon-time.ts` holds the pure functions, tested: place, part of the day, lighting values, red strength
    and moon phase from a `Date`. `HorizonAwake` reads the clock and sets custom properties on the footer, after hydration, so the static
    HTML never holds an hour. `horizon-footer.tsx` draws both bodies; `site.css` reads the properties.
12. **Unchanged:** the ranges, the flag and its raise and wave, the clouds' movement, the stars, the lights, the closing line, the dusk
    edge, every URL and Markdown surface.

## Options considered

| Concern | Option | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whose time | The reader's clock | Matches their own day anywhere; no prompt, nothing sent | A wrong device clock shows a wrong sky | **Chosen by the owner** |
| Whose time | Nepal time | Shows the Himalaya as it is now, the same for everyone | A reader abroad at midday sees night, which can look broken | Rejected |
| Whose time | The reader's location | Real sunrise and sunset | A location prompt on a docs footer, and a privacy decision | Rejected |
| Off hours | Always in the sky, resting low | The theme's body always shows, and its place still tells the time | The sun is visible at midnight, which isn't true to life | **Chosen by the owner** |
| Off hours | True sky, may be empty | Honest to the hour | Half of each day that theme has no sun or moon | Rejected |
| Off hours | One 24-hour arc for both | The simplest; always well up | The moon is highest at noon | Rejected |
| Lighting | Sky, glow, red faces, a soft tint | A real change through the day, keeping the logo's shape | More values to tune in both themes | **Chosen by the owner** |
| Lighting | Glow and red faces only | The cheapest and calmest | Dawn and noon look much alike | Rejected |
| Lighting | Full relight with new lit faces on the left | The most convincing | New geometry that isn't in the logo, on every page | Rejected |
| Look | A natural sun, and a moon in its real phase | Reads as a real sky, with a detail that is true tonight | Leaves the flag's language; new drawing | **Chosen by the owner** |
| Look | The flag's sun and moon | Ties to the site's marks; lightest | Less of a change | Rejected |
| Scroll | Keep the parallax | Keeps what the end of a page does today | The clock's and scroll's positions must combine | **Chosen by the owner** |
| Hour | Set on the client, after hydration | Static pages stay static; correct for each reader | Without scripts there is no hour | Proposed |
| Hour | Rendered on the server | Right on first paint | Needs a timezone the server lacks, and ends prerendering (ADR-0016) | Rejected |
| Hour | An inline script before first paint | No change on screen at load | A second script on every page, for a footer usually out of view at load | Rejected |
| Moon's path | The same simple arc every night | Easy to read, and always up at night | Not where the moon really is | Proposed |
| Moon's path | The real moonrise from an ephemeris | True | Needs a location and more code, and a real moon is often down at night | Rejected |

## Consequences

**Positive**

- The end of every page is different at every hour, and true to the reader's own day and tonight's moon.
- No dependency, no network call, and the pages stay fully static.

**Negative / accepted trade-offs**

- **The star leaves the Horizon.** The star is nepal-dev.com in the site's picture (ADR-0036 decision 5). The home page's system keeps it,
  but in the footer a sun or moon takes its place, and the *star's* words in the living documents change to match.
- **The sky no longer repeats the flag.** ADR-0037 decision 6, the moon over the sun as on the flag, goes, by the owner's choice of a natural
  look. The sun and moon never appear together.
- **It is a clock, not an almanac:** fixed hours, no seasons, and a moon in its true phase on a simplified path. The phase is computed from
  the mean lunar month, so it can be off by up to about half a day.
- The light theme shows a sun at midnight. It rests low and dim, but it is there.
- The observatory's telescope is drawn fixed, so it no longer always points at the light (ADR-0037 decision 9).
- A small jump can show if the footer is already on screen at load, as on the 404, while the script places the body.
- More to look at in review: four parts of the day, in two themes.

## Content invariants

None is touched. The footer is not part of any Markdown surface, and the scene stays `aria-hidden`.

## Security considerations

The device's local time is read in the browser and used only to draw. It is not sent, logged or stored, and the site gains no endpoint,
secret or third party. No location is asked for. Nothing here depends on who the reader is, and no private content is involved.

- [x] `src/lib/horizon-time.ts` with tests: the place at each hour and the crossings; the body always on screen at 320px; every part of the
  day and its blends; red never below 20% and at most 55% under the moon; the phase checked against dates from a published new moon table.
- [x] `horizon-footer.tsx`: both bodies, each in a group placed by the hour around today's `.horizon-rise`. `horizon-sky.ts`: the moon above
  the star goes. `HorizonAwake`: the clock, the minute and the hidden tab.
- [x] `site.css`: which body each theme shows, the lighting properties in both themes, the fade at the crossing, reduced motion.
- [x] Looked at in Chrome against `bunx next build`, with the clock faked: both themes at 6:00, noon, 16:30, 21:15 and 2:10; the widths of
  ADR-0037 decision 11; a theme switch; a new moon and a full moon; no scripts. Not viewed: reduced motion, because the browser tool cannot emulate it, so its rules rest on tests; and a browser without scroll-driven
  animations. Only Chrome was used.
- [x] `bun run test`, `bun run types:check` and `bunx next build` pass; `bun run lint` is reported while it stays blocked.
- Found in the build, within the decisions above:
  - The phase counts from a mean new moon, because a true one is itself up to about half a day off the mean month. Against the seven 2026
    new moons in the test the error is at most 0.56 days.
  - The crossing's fade names the body that jumped, so the moon does not blink at midnight when only the hidden sun moves, nor the sun at noon.
  - The moon's halo and glow scale with how much of it is lit, because a bright halo round a dark disc reads as an eclipse.
  - Clouds' undersides go pale by day, for decision 7's *clear, pale*.
