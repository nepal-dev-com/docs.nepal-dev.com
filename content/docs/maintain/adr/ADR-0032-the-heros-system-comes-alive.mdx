## Context

- The home page's hero is the system (ADR-0017 decision 2): a canvas drawn by
  `src/components/home/orbit-map.tsx`. It has the star, an orbit for each ring that has entries
  (`presentRings` in `src/lib/rings.ts`), the planets with their moons, a tooltip on pointing,
  and the zoom to the Verse (ADR-0029).
- **Today it draws one star, one orbit and one ringed planet.** Only the site's own project is
  documented, so `presentRings` returns the Projects ring alone. The five default orbits appear
  only when the catalogue is empty. On a 1412px screen the system takes the right half of
  the hero and is mostly empty canvas. The owner finds it dull.
- What stands in the way:
  - **The Mark Means rule** (DESIGN.md): a ring means project, a full moon the latest release, a
    crescent an older one, a dashed new moon `dev`, a lock private. None is used for
    decoration, and the legend teaches "dashed = unreleased".
  - **The One Orbit rule** (DESIGN.md): the system is the site's only continuous motion. Its
    exceptions are a diagram's Archify trace (ADR-0022) and the Horizon's sky (ADR-0030).
  - **The still sky** (ADR-0029 decision 3): the page's sky never moves.
  - **The Places, Not Controls rule** (ADR-0029): a space word always stands beside a plain
    sentence.
  - **No invented planets.** The system shows only documented entries; the invented private
    names appear only in the Verse view and beyond the belt (ADR-0016, ADR-0017 decision 4).
- The owner chose, with options, all eight of: every ecosystem's orbit, a living star, planet
  trails, pointer depth, an arrival spin-up, a telemetry line, a comet now and then, and a
  nebula behind the system.

## Decision

The hero's system gains eight things. Everything stays on the existing canvas, or in one still
layer behind it, and uses only the existing tokens. No dependency is added.

1. **Every ecosystem's orbit, waiting.**
   - The system always draws the npm, Composer, PyPI, Go and Projects orbits, the five the
     subline names. It also draws Cargo and Other when an entry is in them, in the existing ring
     order.
   - An orbit with planets is drawn as now.
   - An orbit with none is a thinner, fainter **solid** line: 0.75px, Line 2 mixed with 35% of
     its ecosystem colour, at 60% opacity. It is not dashed, because a dashed mark means
     unreleased.
   - Its name (npm, Composer, PyPI, Go, Projects) sits on the orbit's upper left in 11px Data,
     Steel Faint, and turns with nothing.
   - Pointing within 8px of an empty orbit shows the existing tooltip: its name, "No planets
     yet". It has nothing to open.
   - With planets in only some orbits, the documented planets take their own orbits' radii, so
     the system has the same shape whatever is documented.
2. **A living star.**
   - The disc grows from 9px to 14px, with "Puncoz Nepal" under it as now.
   - Its glow becomes a corona of three soft layers that breathe together over about 6 seconds
     (±15% opacity).
   - Eight thin rays in the star colour at 25% turn once every 120 seconds.
   - This is part of the system's own motion.
3. **Planet trails.** Each planet leaves a trail behind it along its orbit, a sixth of the way
   round: 2px wide, fading from 35% of its colour to nothing (Frost Ink for projects). Moons
   leave none. A trail is drawn from the planet's own position each frame and adds no motion of
   its own.
4. **Pointer depth.**
   - On a device with a fine pointer, the system leans toward the pointer as it moves over the
     hero: the orbits' tilt moves from 0.36 by up to ±0.05, and the centre by up to 8px.
   - The nebula shifts up to 12px the other way. Both ease over about 0.6s, and settle back
     when the pointer leaves.
   - The page's still sky does not move (ADR-0029 decision 3).
   - Off for touch and under reduced motion. A response to input, so the *One Orbit* rule holds.
5. **Arrival: the system spins up.**
   - On the first view of `/` in a session, the star ignites: its disc grows from 40% and its
     corona from nothing, over 0.6s.
   - Each planet starts a third of a turn behind its place and sweeps in along its orbit over
     1.2s with the site's ease; the orbits fade in over 0.4s. Then the system turns as usual.
   - Not again in the same session (remembered in `sessionStorage`; if storage is unavailable,
     it runs each time). Not when `/#verse` is opened, where the zoom out runs instead, and not
     under reduced motion.
   - A single response to arrival.
6. **A telemetry line.**
   - The caption beside *Zoom out to the Verse*, empty in the system view today, reads in 12.5px
     Data, Steel Faint, for example: `1 planet · 4 orbits waiting · 0 moons`.
   - Its counts come from the catalogue: the public entries; the drawn orbits with no planet;
     the version lines, `dev` included. Singular and plural follow the counts.
   - The plain sentence stands with it for screen readers, in place of the data line: "1
     project documented; npm, Composer, PyPI and Go have none yet; no versions released." On
     screen the legend under the search button names the marks.
   - In the Verse view the caption reads, as now, *The Verse · one system so far*.
7. **A comet now and then: the One Orbit rule's third exception.**
   - Every 20 to 30 seconds, on a seeded schedule, a comet crosses the upper part of the
     system: a 2px Frost Ink head with a 90px tail that fades. The tail always points away from
     the star, the way a comet's does.
   - It is in view for about 2.5 seconds.
   - It never crosses the text column. On desktop it stays right of 52% of the width; on phones
     it stays inside the system's 310px.
   - It runs only while the hero is on screen, like the system. It doesn't run in the Verse view
     or under reduced motion.
   - It is not a mark, and it carries no meaning.
8. **A nebula behind the system.**
   - A still layer behind the canvas: two soft radial washes centred on the star, Teal at 8% and
     the star colour at 6% in dark, half that in light. They reach 1.4 times the outer orbit.
   - It is masked away from the text column (left of 50% on desktop; outside the system's 310px
     on phones), so nothing glows behind text.
   - It moves only with pointer depth, and shrinks with the system in the Verse view.
9. **Restraint.**
   - Everything new is quieter than the planets. The only new labels are the empty orbits'
     names.
   - The new continuous motion is the star's breathing and rays, inside the system, and the
     rare comet.
   - Under reduced motion the system is drawn once, as now, with the corona, the trails, the
     empty orbits and the nebula still. Without scripts there is no canvas and nothing changes.
10. **Unchanged:** the headline, subline, search button, legend, tooltip on planets, clicking a
    planet, the Verse view and its words, the morph, the still sky, every URL and every Markdown
    surface.

## Options considered

| Question | Option | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Objects | Every ecosystem's orbit | Structure today; honest about scope; fills naturally | Five orbits with one planet still reads as early days | Chosen (owner) |
| Objects | A living star | The centre is the maker; costs a few draws | One more motion, inside the system | Chosen (owner) |
| Objects | Planet trails | The turning reads at a glance | Busier with many planets | Chosen (owner) |
| Objects | Pointer depth | Responsive and alive on desktop; no loop | Desktop only | Chosen (owner) |
| Around it | Arrival spin-up | A first-screen moment, once | 1.2s before the system settles | Chosen (owner) |
| Around it | A telemetry line | Honest numbers in the site's technical voice | One more line in the hero | Chosen (owner) |
| Around it | A comet now and then | The most alive | A third loop exception; it means nothing | Chosen (owner) |
| Around it | A nebula | Depth behind the system | Decorative; near the glow cliché | Chosen (owner), kept faint and masked from text |
| Empty orbits | Dashed, as first offered | Reads as "waiting" | The legend says dashed = unreleased: breaks *Mark Means* | Rejected for a fainter solid line |
| Telemetry | Plain words only ("1 project · 0 packages") | No space words | Less of the system's voice | Rejected; the plain sentence is the screen-reader text |
| Objects | A moon or ring as decoration | Familiar | Breaks *Mark Means* | Not offered |

## Consequences

**Positive**

- The first screen shows the whole shape of the site today, and grows into it as packages arrive.
- The system has a living centre, visible motion and depth, without a second always-on loop
  except the rare comet.

**Negative / accepted trade-offs**

- A third exception to the *One Orbit* rule: a comet with no meaning crosses the hero every 20
  to 30 seconds.
- The canvas code grows (`orbit-map.tsx` is already 443 lines); the new geometry moves to a
  library file so it can be tested.
- On first visit the system takes 1.2s to settle; the text and search button are usable at once.
- The nebula is decoration; it is kept faint and away from text.

## Content invariants

None is touched. No content is added; the telemetry counts come from the public catalogue.
Per-page Markdown, `llms.txt` and `llms-full.txt` are unchanged (§6.5).

## Security considerations

None. This change touches no data, no endpoint and no secret. It reads only the public
catalogue the page already has. `sessionStorage` holds one flag, "the hero has spun up", with
no personal data.

## Implementation notes

- [x] `src/lib/hero-sky.ts`, plain functions so they can be tested:
  - `heroRings(entries)`: the five orbits always, plus Cargo and Other when present, in ring
    order;
  - `systemFacts(entries)`: the telemetry counts, the data line and the plain sentence;
    the sentence ends "no versions documented yet" rather than decision 6's "no versions
    released", because a `dev` line is a moon the count includes but is not a release;
  - `cometStarts(seed, n)`: when each comet starts, 20 to 30 seconds apart (named for what it
    returns, in place of `cometSchedule`);
  - `cometPath(...)`: its path inside the system region, never left of the text boundary nor
    above a `top` line, its tail pointing away from the star. The canvas passes `top: 72`, so a
    comet stays below the site header, which the hero's canvas runs under;
  - `trailArc(...)`: a planet's trail as an arc behind it;
  - `spinUp(t)`: the arrival's eased offset and the star's, corona's and orbits' light;
  - also `corona(t)`, `lean(nx, ny)`, `orbitDistance(...)` for the empty orbits' tooltip, and
    `mixHex(...)` for their colour.
- [x] `src/lib/rings.ts`: the hero uses `heroRings`. `presentRings` and `emptyRings` were used
  only by the hero, so they are removed; `ringOrder` is exported for `heroRings`.
- [x] `src/components/home/orbit-map.tsx`: empty orbits and their labels and tooltip, the star's
  corona and rays, trails, pointer depth, the arrival, the comet, all in the existing draw loop
  and its pause, reduced-motion and hidden-tab handling; the caption shows the telemetry line.
  With five orbits the system's size is capped on desktop so it stays clear of the text, and a
  planet's label flips to its left where it would leave the canvas.
- [x] `src/app/(home)/home.css`: the nebula layer, its mask and its pointer shift; the caption.
- [x] Tests (ADR-0007, ADR-0021) for `hero-sky.ts` (`src/lib/hero-sky.test.ts`):
  - the five orbits always, with extra rings only when present;
  - the counts, plurals and plain sentence, including an empty catalogue;
  - comets 20 to 30 seconds apart, always right of the text boundary and below the header,
    tails away from the star;
  - trails behind the planet and a sixth of a turn long;
  - the spin-up ending exactly at zero offset.
- [x] The canvas is a client component and is not unit-tested (ADR-0021). Checked in Chrome
  against `bunx next build` and the development server, at about 1412px and 390px, in both
  themes: the empty orbits and tooltip, the star, trails, pointer depth, the arrival once per
  session, the comet (sampled from the canvas: nothing drawn under the header), the nebula's
  mask, the caption, the Verse view; reduced motion in the code.
- [x] `bun run test`, `bun run types:check` and `bunx next build` pass; `bun run lint` is reported
  while it stays blocked.
