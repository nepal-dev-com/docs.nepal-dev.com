## Context

The site has a Verse name, as in universe (ADR-0028). When the owner chose the name, they
asked for the whole site to carry that theme, and ADR-0028 left the theme to this ADR.

The solar system is already on every page (ADR-0017, ADR-0018, ADR-0027): the star is the maker,
entries are planets, version lines are moons, Use and Maintain are a planet's day and night sides,
and the sidebar is a scale ladder down to the page. What the site does not have:

- **A level above the system.** The home page shows one system and nothing around it, so the
  name *Verse* has no place on the map.
- **A mark of its own.** The header's brand mark is a ringed planet, and under DESIGN.md's
  *Mark Means* rule a ring means a project.
- **A sky on reading pages.** Docs pages are plain graphite; the space feeling lives on the home
  page, the 404 and the footer only.
- **A sense of scale between pages.** Moving from the home page to a planet, or from a page back
  up to its planet, is an ordinary page change. Fumadocs' breadcrumb names the folders but none of
  the levels above them.

Facts that shape the options:

- React 19.3 ships `<ViewTransition>` and `addTransitionType`, and Next.js 16.4 runs App Router
  navigations as transitions with no configuration; `<Link>` takes `transitionTypes`
  (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/02-guides/view-transitions.md`, `…/02-components/link.md`).
  Browsers without the View Transitions API navigate normally.
- Fumadocs' notebook page takes its breadcrumb as a slot (`slots.breadcrumb` on `DocsPage`).
- The site has no favicon or app icon (`src/app/` holds no `icon` file).
- DESIGN.md's *One Orbit* rule: the turning system is the only continuous motion of the site's
  own.

The owner chose on 2026-11-01:

- all three parts of the theme, **staged**: identity and sky, then zoom, then words, each
  built and reviewed as its own step;
- **one system, a wider sky** at the top level;
- the approach **still sky, morph zoom**.

## Decision

1. **The Verse is the sky around the one system.** ADR-0017's metaphor stands: the star is the
   maker, and every public entry orbits it. Above the system there is only sky, the asteroid belt
   and, beyond it, the private projects, dim and far. The site has one scale ladder, from the top:
   **Verse** (the site) → **System** (the home page) → **Planet** (an entry's overview) → **Side
   and moon** (a track and its version line) → **Orbit** (a folder) → **Satellite** (a page) →
   **Probe** (where the reader is).

### Stage 1: identity and sky

2. **The Verse mark.** A new brand mark replaces the ringed planet in the header and on Open
   Graph images, and becomes the site's icon (`src/app/icon.svg`, Next.js' file convention): the
   system seen from outside, a star-coloured centre inside two nested, tilted orbits in Frost Ink
   with a teal planet on the inner one. It has no ring, so it never reads as a project. The
   wordmark keeps its face and size; the name stays. The final drawing is reviewed
   with stage 1.
3. **A still sky on every page.** The home page, docs pages and the 404 stand on one star field:
   - drawn on the server as SVG from a fixed seed, so it is the same on every load and needs no
     script;
   - still: it never moves, twinkles or drifts (the *One Orbit* rule holds);
   - three sizes of star (about 0.6, 1 and 1.6px) in Steel Faint and Frost Ink at low opacity,
     thickest at the page's edges and behind the header, thinning toward the content;
   - **never behind text**: none under the reading column, the sidebar's rows or the table of
     contents, so every contrast measured in DESIGN.md stays as it is;
   - in the light theme, the same field as fine graphite specks at lower opacity, like a printed
     star chart.
   The home page's turning system draws over the sky, with its own stars removed so the two don't
   double.

### Stage 2: zoom

4. **The scale strip.** On every docs page Fumadocs' breadcrumb is replaced by a strip naming
   each level with its mark: `Verse ▸ System ▸ ● lodestar ▸ ☀ Use · ◯ v0.1 ▸ ○ Guides ▸ Forms`.
   - *Verse* links to `/#verse`, *System* to `/`, the planet to its overview, the side and moon to
     the line's first page, the orbit to its index when it has one (otherwise it is plain text);
     the current page is plain text with `aria-current="page"`.
   - It is a `nav` labelled *Scale*. On phones it shows only the parent level, with a back arrow,
     and the current page's name.
   - An overview shows `Verse ▸ System ▸ ● planet`. Maintain shows a moon for the side and no
     version line.
5. **The Verse view.** The home page's system gets one control at its lower right, *Zoom out to
   the Verse*. Pressing it, or opening `/#verse`, eases the camera out over about 1.2 seconds with
   the site's ease: the system shrinks to the centre, still turning, the wider sky opens around it,
   the belt and the private planets show dim and far, labels fade except the maker's, and a
   caption reads *The Verse · one system so far*. The control becomes *Back to the system*;
   pressing it, or the system itself, eases back in. It is a single response to input, so the
   *One Orbit* rule holds.
6. **The morph.** Opening a planet from its home page card morphs the card's planet into the
   overview's planet header (a shared `<ViewTransition>` named for the entry), and coming back
   morphs it back. Links in the scale strip and the sidebar's planet chip carry a transition type:
   going up a level the new page settles in from slightly larger (zoom out), going down from
   slightly smaller (zoom in), in about 0.35 seconds.
7. **Motion off means no motion.** With reduced motion the Verse view changes instantly and no
   page morphs or zooms; in a browser without view transitions, navigation is unchanged. Nothing
   is hidden before scripts run.

### Stage 3: words

8. **Space words name places, never controls.** Controls and the labels readers scan for keep
   their plain names: Search, Overview, Use, Maintain, On this page, version names, Back to top.
   Space words go where the site describes itself: empty states, hints, the scale strip's top two
   levels, the Verse view, the 404 and the footer, always alongside a plain sentence. The copy
   that changes is listed in the app flow's *Words* table; DESIGN.md gains the vocabulary as a
   named rule.

### How it is built

9. **One stage at a time, reviewed between.** Stage 1 is built on approval. Stage 2 starts once
   the owner has reviewed stage 1 in the working tree, and stage 3 once they have reviewed stage
   2. This departs from `AGENTS.md` §3 ("don't stop for per-change review") by the owner's
   choice. Each stage ships its tests, DESIGN.md, the app flow's status and the docs below.
10. **Unchanged:** the marks and their meanings, the scale ladder in the sidebar (ADR-0027), the
    search button's text, every URL, and every Markdown surface: the sky, the strip, the zoom and
    the morph are only drawn on the page, so per-page Markdown, `llms.txt` and `llms-full.txt`
    stay the same.

## Options considered

| Concern | Option | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scope | A — identity and a quiet sky | Smallest change seen everywhere | The name still has no place on the map | Rejected (owner) |
| Scope | B — zoom between scales | The hierarchy becomes something you move through | A navigation element and transitions | Rejected alone (owner) |
| Scope | C — words only | Cheap | Clarity risk on a docs site; nothing visible | Rejected alone (owner) |
| Scope | D — all three, staged | The whole theme, reviewed in steps | Largest; three reviews | Chosen (owner) |
| Top level | A — one system, a wider sky | ADR-0017 stays true; nothing changes meaning | The top level is atmosphere more than navigation | Chosen (owner) |
| Top level | B — ecosystems as star systems, the maker as the galaxy's core | Real navigation at the top | Supersedes ADR-0017's metaphor; redraws the home page | Rejected (owner) |
| Top level | C — a public and a private system | Prepares sign-in | Decides how private work shows, which needs its own ADR | Rejected (owner) |
| Top level | D — no level above the system | Smallest | *Verse* is only a name | Rejected (owner) |
| Approach | A — still sky, morph zoom | Keeps the *One Orbit* rule; reading pages stay server-rendered; no new library | The sky never moves | Chosen (owner) |
| Approach | B — a living sky with a camera zoom | Most immersive | A second continuous motion; a client canvas on every reading page | Rejected (owner) |
| Approach | C — zoom as a place only | Safest for motion | The zoom is never seen happening | Rejected (owner) |
| Sky on reading pages | A — around the content, never behind text | Contrast unchanged | Fewer stars on narrow screens | Chosen |
| Sky on reading pages | B — everywhere, dimmed behind text | Fuller sky | Every text pair re-measured; busier reading | Rejected |
| Phone strip | A — parent level and current page | Fits 360px; one tap up | The full ladder is only on wider screens | Chosen |
| Phone strip | B — the full strip, scrolled sideways | Every level | Horizontal scrolling inside the page | Rejected |

## Consequences

**Positive**

- The name has a place: the Verse is the first level of the ladder and a view on the home page.
- Every page shares one sky and one mark, so the site reads as one place.
- Readers see where they are in the ladder on every page, and moving up or down it shows as a
  zoom.
- The brand mark no longer borrows the project mark.

**Negative / accepted trade-offs**

- Three stages and three reviews before the theme is complete.
- The star field adds an inline SVG to every page (a few kilobytes).
- The morph works only between a home page card and its overview; the canvas planets can't be
  part of a view transition.
- The Verse view is mostly atmosphere until there is more than one system to show.

## Content invariants

None touched. No page moves and no URL changes. The sky, the strip, the Verse view and the morph
are drawn on the page only, so every Markdown surface stays as it is (§6.5). The private planets
in the Verse view are the three invented names already on the home page (ADR-0017); no private
content is added.

## Security considerations

None. This change touches no data, no endpoint and no secret. `/#verse` is a fragment read in the
browser; it never reaches the server and carries nothing about the reader. No new third party is
involved and no dependency is added.

      stars, so nothing there changed; the home wrapper's own ground fill, which hid the sky, is
      removed (the body paints the same colour). The header's band is `HeaderSky`, evenly spread
      and masked to the gap between the links and the buttons.
- [x] Tests (ADR-0007, ADR-0021): the field is the same for the same seed and different for
      another; no star falls in a cleared region; star counts and sizes stay in their ranges; the
      mark renders with no ring and with its title for assistive tech.
- [x] Checked in Chrome on localhost against `bunx next build`, dark and light: home and a docs page

      band at 1600px and at 570px (clear of the wordmark and the buttons), the 404, the Open Graph
      image and the icon link. Where the content fills the width there is no page sky, only the
      header's band. `impeccable detect` over the changed files: one advisory, the existing 11px
      profile chip (ADR-0020).

**Stage 2: zoom**

- [x] `src/components/docs/scale-strip.tsx` and `src/lib/scale-strip.ts`: the levels from the
      page tree and the catalogue (decision 4). Fumadocs' `slots.breadcrumb` receives only its
      own breadcrumb options and its `component` option is deprecated, so the page turns the
      breadcrumb off (`breadcrumb={{ enabled: false }}`) and renders the strip as its first
      child, in the same place. A side level links to the first page of its line or track; an
      orbit links to its folder's index when it has one.
- [x] `src/components/home/orbit-map.tsx` with `src/lib/verse-view.ts`: the Verse view, its
      control and caption, and `#verse` (decision 5). The far planets sit up-right, right and
      below the star, so they never reach the hero's text; a click on the canvas in the Verse
      view zooms back in.
- [x] `src/components/zoom-page.tsx`: `ZoomPage` around each page (the home page and every docs
      page) and `PlanetMorph` on the card's and the planet header's planet (decision 6).
      `src/lib/zoom.ts` holds the two transition types, apart from `scale-strip.ts` so client
      components do not pull in the catalogue's build checks. Zoom-out on the strip's links and
      the planet chip; zoom-in on the card's name and on the Use and Maintain links of the card
      and the planet header. The CSS for both directions and for reduced motion is in
      `src/app/global.css` (decision 7).
- [x] Tests: the strip's levels for an overview, a Use page in a line, a Maintain page, a page in
      a nested folder, a folder's index and a folder without one, a page outside the catalogue;
      the phone level; the Verse view's easing, scale and far planets; the wrappers add no
      markup.
- [x] Checked in Chrome on localhost against `bunx next build`: the strip at 1600px and 570px
      (links and their targets, the phone's parent level and back arrow), the Verse view in and
      out by the button and by a click, `/#verse` easing out on load, and the morph from the card
      to the planet header with the `zoom-in` type. Between two docs pages the page zooms out;
      between the home page and a docs page the route groups' layouts differ, so the page itself
