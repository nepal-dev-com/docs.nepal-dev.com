## Context

- Every page ends with the full Horizon footer (ADR-0018 decision 4): the closing line, a scene
  of the star rising behind a planet's curved edge, then the planet row, the link row and the
  credit. `src/components/horizon-footer.tsx` draws the scene as one still SVG: 60 seeded stars,
  the star's glow and disc, the planet's ground and its teal atmosphere rim.
- **On docs pages the sidebar runs over it.** Fumadocs' notebook layout puts the sidebar in a
  sticky wrapper (`z-index: 20`) as tall as the window, and the trajectory ("On this page") in a
  sticky column. The footer is a full-width grid row with no stacking of its own, so at the end
  of a page the sidebar's cards and the trajectory sit on top of the closing line and the scene.
  Checked in Chrome at 1632px on `/docs/maintain/prd/diagrams`.
- The owner finds the scene not lively enough, and asked for clouds and other sky objects, with
  animation where it helps, without making it worse.
- What stands in the way:
  - DESIGN.md's *One Orbit* rule: the turning system is the site's only continuous motion. Its one
    exception is a diagram's Archify trace (ADR-0022).
  - ADR-0015 decision 7: no entrance motion on docs pages.
  - DESIGN.md's *Mark Means* rule: a moon means a version line and a ring means a project. So
    the scene gets no moon and no ringed planet.
  - ADR-0029 decision 3: the page's still sky never moves. That sky lies behind the page; the
    scene is the footer's own sky and is not part of it.
- The owner chose, with options: the horizon **rises over** the page; a **gentle loop** of
  motion; and four objects, **lit clouds, a shooting star, an aurora on the rim and an
  observatory on the ridge**.

## Decision

1. **The horizon rises over the page.** On docs pages the footer stacks above the sidebar and
   the trajectory, under the site header (`z-index: 30`, the header is 40), on an opaque sky.
   - Its top edge is a **dusk edge**: 96px fading from transparent to the ground colour, so
     the sidebar and the trajectory sink into dusk where the horizon meets them, rather than
     being cut off by a line.
   - As the footer comes into view, the sidebar and the trajectory **set**: they drop up to 24px
     and dim to 55%, in step with the scroll. Scrolling back up raises them again.
   - What lies above the horizon stays where it was and works as before. What lies below it is
     hidden until the reader scrolls up.
   - On phones the sidebar is a drawer and the trajectory a bar under the header, so nothing
     overlaps. The home page and the 404 have neither.
2. **The scene is a dusk sky with five layers**, back to front, the same on every page:
   - **Stars:** the 60 seeded stars stay. Fourteen of them twinkle, between 25% and full
     opacity over 3 to 7 seconds each, with staggered starts.
   - **Aurora:** two soft teal ribbons rising off the atmosphere rim, drawn as blurred SVG
     paths. They breathe over about 9 seconds (opacity 0.25 to 0.55, a slight vertical
     stretch), and the rim brightens with them.
   - **Shooting stars:** a thin streak with a fading tail crosses the upper sky, down and to the
     left, visible for about a second. There are two, on 14- and 23-second cycles, so they never
     fall into a beat.
   - **The star** rises with the scroll: from 40px below the curve, as the footer starts to
     enter, to its place when the footer is half in view, its glow growing with it.
   - **Lit clouds:** three thin bands at different heights, each a seamless strip that loops
     sideways over 90, 140 and 200 seconds. Their undersides catch the star's colour and their
     tops fade into the ground; the lowest band passes in front of the star's glow, never its
     disc.
   - **The observatory:** a small silhouette on the ridge, left of the star: a domed building
     standing on the planet's curve, its slit open toward the star, a telescope line in it and
     one warm window. It does not move.
3. **The rules for its motion.** The scene is the *One Orbit* rule's second named exception,
   beside Archify:
   - Only transform and opacity animate, in CSS, with no library (ADR-0014 decision 7). The blur
     is drawn once and never animated.
   - Loops run only while the footer is on screen. A small client component,
     `HorizonAwake`, sets `data-awake` on the footer through an `IntersectionObserver`; without it,
     every loop is paused. Browsers already pause them in hidden tabs.
   - The rise and the setting are scroll-driven (`animation-timeline`, named
     `--horizon` on the footer and scoped to the page). Where a browser does not support that,
     the star stands risen, and the sidebar and the trajectory are simply covered by the dusk
     edge.
   - Under reduced motion nothing loops, rises or sets. The star stands risen, the clouds stay
     where they are drawn, and the sidebar is covered as in the fallback.
   - Nothing moves behind text: the closing line and the surface rows stand on the opaque
     ground, above the scene.
4. **Light theme.** The clouds turn pale with warm undersides, the aurora drops to half its
   opacity, the stars use Steel Faint at lower opacity, and the ground is the light ground.
5. **Unchanged:** the closing line, the planet row, the link row and its labels ("Back to top" on
   docs pages, "Back to orbit" elsewhere), the credit, the page's still sky (ADR-0029), every
   URL and every Markdown surface. The scene stays `aria-hidden` inline SVG in the existing
   tokens (ADR-0017 decision 13). No dependency is added.

## Options considered

| Question | Option | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overlap | A — the horizon rises over the page | Keeps the full-width scene; the end of the page becomes a moment; reuses the planet's own edge | The bottom of the sidebar is hidden while the footer is in view | Chosen (owner) |
| Overlap | B — the sidebar lands on the horizon | Conventional; no new motion | The sidebar and trajectory scroll away abruptly; nothing of the theme | Rejected |
| Overlap | C — the horizon fits the reading column | No overlap at all | A narrow, cramped scene; two footer widths to keep | Rejected |
| Motion | A — a moment on arrival, then a slow drift | Calm; one small exception | Less lively than asked | Rejected |
| Motion | B — a moment on arrival only | Keeps the *One Orbit* rule | The scene goes still again at once | Rejected |
| Motion | C — a gentle loop | The liveliest; reads as a real sky | Motion at the end of every page; a second exception to *One Orbit* | Chosen (owner) |
| Objects | Lit clouds, a shooting star, an aurora on the rim, an observatory on the ridge | Depth, life and a story beat, with no mark that carries meaning | More drawing and CSS in the footer | Chosen (owner), all four |
| Objects | A moon, a ringed planet | Familiar sky objects | Break the *Mark Means* rule: moons are version lines, rings are projects | Rejected |

## Consequences

**Positive**

- The sidebar and the trajectory no longer sit on the closing line and the scene; they set
  below the horizon, which belongs to the site's own world.
- The end of every page has a living sky that rewards reaching it.
- The motion stays cheap and stops when nobody is looking: CSS transforms and opacity, paused off
  screen and in hidden tabs, and gone under reduced motion.

**Negative / accepted trade-offs**

- While the footer is in view, the bottom of the sidebar is hidden; scrolling up brings it back.
  On a page shorter than the window the footer is in view on arrival, and the sidebar stands
  partly below the horizon.
- The scene moves at the end of every page, including docs pages, which ADR-0015 kept still.
- Firefox, without scroll-driven animations by default, shows the risen star and no setting.
- The footer's SVG and CSS grow; they ship with every page.

## Content invariants

None is touched. The scene is decoration in the footer: per-page Markdown, `llms.txt` and
`llms-full.txt` are unchanged (§6.5), and no content is added.

## Security considerations

None. This change touches no data, no endpoint and no secret. The scene is inline SVG and
CSS with no external resource. The new client component reads only whether the footer is on
screen.

## Implementation notes

- [x] `src/lib/horizon-sky.ts`:
  - the scene's stars, from `starField` in `src/lib/sky.ts` (seeded, as now), with the
    twinkling subset and each one's duration and delay;
  - the cloud bands' shapes;
  - `ridgeY(x)`, the height of the planet's curve at a point, so the observatory stands on it.
- [x] `src/components/horizon-footer.tsx`: the layers in decision 2, in order, inside the
  `aria-hidden` scene. Every looping layer is its own element, so its transform or opacity is
  composited and the blurs are drawn once. The cloud bands are sized in container units to the
  viewBox the other layers show, so sliding a band by half its width moves it exactly one period
  at any width.
- [x] `src/components/horizon-awake.tsx`: the client component in decision 3. It sets
  `data-awake` while the footer is on screen and removes it when it leaves.
- [x] `src/app/site.css`:
  - the footer's stacking, opaque ground and dusk edge on docs pages;
  - the layers, their keyframes and the light theme;
  - the scroll-driven rise and setting inside `@supports (animation-timeline: view())`;
  - `timeline-scope: --horizon` on the root;
  - paused loops without `data-awake`;
  - the reduced-motion rules.
- [x] Tests (ADR-0007, ADR-0021):
  - the stars are the same on every build and the twinkling count is fourteen;
  - `ridgeY` lies on the curve at the centre and at the observatory's place;
  - the scene renders every layer in order, stays `aria-hidden` and has nothing to focus;
  - the clouds loop seamlessly (each puff inside its period) and never cross the star's disc.
  `HorizonAwake` is a client component, not unit-tested until a DOM is chosen. The surface rows
  are unchanged and not rendered in a test, because their Search button reads Fumadocs' search
  context. Both were checked in Chrome instead.
- [x] Checked in Chrome on localhost against `bunx next build`:
  - a docs page at about 1600px: the horizon over the sidebar and the trajectory, the setting
    and the rise both ways with the scroll;
  - the loops running only while the footer is on screen;
  - the home page, the light theme, and phone width;
  - the 404 ends with the same Horizon, but it still renders inside the docs layout at unknown
    paths, and that layout's header covers it (an existing bug, unchanged here);
  - reduced motion in the CSS, not by emulating the setting.
- [x] `bun run test`, `bun run types:check` and `bunx next build` pass; `bun run lint` is reported
  while it stays blocked.
